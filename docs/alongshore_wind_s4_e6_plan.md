Approval of the first gate would permit an availability and support audit. A later modelling run depends on review of the actual counts and release-time evidence. ## Forecast and contrast

For a drifter already in a known 1° release cell at time $t_0$, does knowing recent alongshore wind improve its stopped destination distribution at 7 or 30 days? Compare annual B2 with one wind-conditioned version of the same row. Select the adjustment separately by horizon. Use the qualified v7 pooled population in release polygon R, fixed transport domain D, geographic destinations plus absorbing first exit E, and the existing direct labels. Both forecasts know the same origin cell. Wind is the only additional input. A fresh release does not need a previous position or a known heading.

Near Cape Hatteras, moorings linked along-shelf transport variability to wind and transport convergence to Gulf Stream position. Those observations motivate the signed wind component. They do not establish a predictive effect throughout R. A wind-conditioned row can be associated with current changes or the drogue composition of the pooled sample. It does not estimate direct windage of a particular material. [Savidge & Bane](https://digitalcommons.odu.edu/ccpo_pubs/268/) · [Project domain and target](../README.md)

## Gate A · Can we reconstruct what the forecaster could know?

**First-choice source.** NCEP FNL operational global analyses, distributed by NSF NCAR GDEX, dataset d083002, DOI 10.5065/D6M043C6. The archive spans 2001–2022 on a 1° grid with analyses every six hours. Request only earth-relative eastward and northward winds at 10 m above the surface, in m/s, and the grid metadata for the neighbourhood of R. The catalogue records a GRIB1/GRIB2 change in December 2007. Audit both eras, units, orientation, level, file completeness and original operational vintages. A regional subset or indexed byte-range retrieval is required for bulk work. [Archive and inventory](https://gdex.ucar.edu/datasets/d083002/) · [NCEP field selector](https://nomads.ncep.noaa.gov/gribfilter.php?ds=fnl)

**As-of rule.** Every wind cycle needs both its UTC valid time and evidence that the *same vintage* had been publicly disseminated by $t_0$. Accept a timestamped historical operational release or contemporaneous receipt, or a documented conservative upper bound on actual availability covering that cycle, version and exceptions. Archive ingestion is usable as an upper bound only with a contemporaneous receipt for that exact version. A usual schedule, assumed six-hour lag, present-day file modification time or valid time alone cannot qualify a cycle. FNL is produced with a delay to ingest further observations. Save evidence type, time or upper bound, URI, original filename, version and checksum alongside each accepted cycle. Reject revised fields unless that revision was already available by departure. ERA5 is useful for retrospective science but its ordinary five-day preliminary latency and later revision disqualify same-day ERA5 winds as operational inputs here. [FNL documentation](https://gdex.ucar.edu/datasets/d083002/) · [ECMWF latency](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/forecasts/datasets/era5-hourly-data-single-levels-1940-present)

Start with metadata and representative cycles in 2001, on both sides of the 2007 format transition, and in 2019 and 2022. Verify a feasible receipt trail before retrieving the full subset. A failed timing check marks the cycle unavailable. If large eras have no provable receipt trail, report the affected dates and sample exposure. Stop at feasibility review; any alternative product, later start date or retrospective question needs a revised contract.

## One wind feature, calculated the same way for both models' release cases

1. **Extraction location.** Fix one source node per 1° release cell. Use the closest ocean grid node to its centre within 100 km (WGS84, ties by latitude then longitude), using the project's frozen high-resolution land mask for the ocean-node lookup. Record the cell centre, chosen node, distance and ambiguity near land. A missing ocean node yields unavailable wind. All releases in the same cell and time share their wind, which can also be the same in adjacent cells. Future drifter positions never enter the lookup.
2. **Alongshore axis.** For a reproducible regional approximation, use this predeclared mainland-coast guide ordered poleward. The ordered vertices (longitude, latitude) are (−81.40, 30.40), (−80.85, 31.99), (−79.85, 32.75), (−78.00, 33.85), (−76.55, 34.60), (−75.55, 35.25). At each cell centre take the nearest finite WGS84 geodesic segment (tie to the southern segment), and its forward azimuth at the nearest point. Project the wind in local east/north coordinates, positive toward the northern end. Plot the arrows over the coastline before modelling. These are proposed directional anchors, not surveyed coast points or a reconstructed Gulf Stream axis. A single regional coastal bearing in an offshore cell is a proxy.
3. **Time window.** Consider the four scheduled six-hourly valid times in $(t_0-24\,\mathrm{h},t_0]$. Retain only cycles available by $t_0$, with finite and QC-valid components. Require at least three consecutive slots and the newest retained value no older than 12 h. Average the qualifying vectors equally. No temporal interpolation from a later cycle or wind encountered along the future trajectory. Report three- versus four-slot coverage and newest/oldest age. For a 10:00 UTC release, the slots are the previous day's 12:00 and 18:00 and today's 00:00 and 06:00. A 06:00 field released at 10:30 is excluded even when the archive contains it today.
4. **Direction and strength.** With coast azimuth $a_i$ clockwise from true north, define $w=\bar u\sin a_i+\bar v\cos a_i$, in m/s. Use wind *toward* the indicated direction. Freeze three categories: $w\ge 3$ poleward, $w\le-3$ equatorward, and $-3<w<3$ weak net alongshore component. The last category can include strong cross-shore wind or reversing winds. Keep unavailable separate. Publish $w$, the mean scalar wind speed, and their distributions; only the three-level category enters the candidate. The 24 h window, 3 m/s cutoff, guide and lookup radius are design choices proposed before scoring, not published effect thresholds.

These are feasibility thresholds, not power guarantees. Failing a gate yields a support report for that horizon. Outer covariate counts can be reported blind to labels but may not tune thresholds, alter inclusion, or choose an estimator. Preserve the entire original roster.

## Matched estimator and selection

Let $A_{ih}$ be the ordinary B2 row from all qualified training pairs, and $R_{ihr}$ the empirical row from the same origin/horizon using only pairs in wind category $r$. For rows passing the gate issue

$$
P_{ihr}=(1-\lambda_h)A_{ih}+\lambda_h R_{ihr},\qquad
\lambda_h\in\{0,0.25,0.50,0.75\}.
$$

Raw pairs fit both rows, including E. Missing donor winds still contribute to annual B2. A missing query wind or unsupported category uses $A_{ih}$. If the annual row itself is unsupported, both forecasts receive the existing common unsupported status, with counts and weight reported. The cap retains positive mass on every annual-supported outcome. This experiment asks whether wind can redistribute that mass. Choose one parameter per horizon from pooled out-of-fold predictions, using the original averaging hierarchy across the two windows and smallest mean Brier, tie within $10^{-12}$ to the smaller parameter. Refit only on the outer training role; freeze hashes, parameters, fallback flags, entire probability vectors and region masks before outer scoring. Keep any comparisons to heading or spatial borrowing descriptive and separate from the primary contrast.

## Verification, metrics and interpretation

- Carry over the E4 evaluation weights: mean over times per drifter/origin, then equally across drifters in that origin, then equally across origins. Score wind and annual predictions on the identical cases. Include fallback cases, with zero paired differences. Use standard multiclass Brier $1+\sum_j p_j^2-2p_y$. Use the existing deterministic tie rules to accumulate at least 90% of forecast mass. H90 is observed membership including E; K90 counts geographic cells only. Report issued mass, true-outcome zero mass, fallback reasons and denominators. [E4 evaluation and benchmark](departure_direction_s4_e4_results.md)
- Primary inference uses 10,000 shared whole-ID multiplier draws conditional on the fitted forecasts, with one-sided per-horizon alpha 0.025, as in E4. Development evidence of improved Brier requires the corrected upper bound for wind minus annual below zero. Require H90 point difference at least zero and K90 point difference at most zero to call it a gain with both quality point checks passed. Report intervals for H90 and K90. Claim statistically supported preservation only when the corresponding bounds also exclude deterioration. Always distinguish a point screen from demonstrated non-degradation. Examine time-block contributions and leave-one-validation-year-out summaries as descriptive sensitivity to shared weather events. These do not replace the primary test or provide independent storms.
- Reproduce annual scores on the same dataset: Brier 0.995653 / 1.010730, H90 75.015% / 49.422%, K90 18.167 / 61.029 for 7 / 30 d. Verify the timestamp boundary, wind sign/reversal (including exact ±3), weak crosswind, land-node rejection, missing slots, eligible ID counts, probability mass, exact equivalence at zero weight, identical B2 fallback, stopping/exit handling and the two-stage no-label-use boundary. Audit from raw cycle manifests and sample donor counts independently of production aggregates.
- Deliver the source/time manifest, map of wind axes/nodes, regime-by-cell unique-ID table, coverage and fallback plot, season-by-wind table, training selection curves, and paired Brier/H90/K90 results by horizon. Save detailed science outputs to a separate E6 run and preview. Current validation has prior development exposure; the locked final test remains sealed.

**Proposed budgets and decision.** For the initial receipt/inventory check cap retrieval at 100 MiB. The full support audit requires demonstrably subsettable data and a preflight estimate below 2 GiB transferred and 4 GiB of new local storage, with a 2-hour active processing limit. Report transfer/job-queue delays separately. Pause before crossing limits. After support review a formal run would use a pilot, primary and independent reconstruction within 60 cumulative active compute minutes excluding prior acquisition. If the wind comparison passes, evaluate whether season can substitute for observed winds in a *separate* proposal. If wind helps only at seven days, limit the conclusion to seven days. Weak coverage or support yields a feasibility conclusion, not evidence that winds have no effect.
