## 6. Apply explicit missing-value and structural rules

### Identifiers and timestamps

Use the official GDP ID, not an interchangeable WMO identifier. Preserve integer precision; never pass identifiers through floating-point conversion.

Use canonical positive decimal ID strings where strings are required.

Decode timestamps using the source units and calendar. Store unambiguous UTC times. Do not round irregular timestamps onto the hour to manufacture regularity.

A missing or undecodable timestamp remains traceable through its source key, but cannot provide a dated origin, season or transition endpoint. Do not invent its time.

Sort records within each ID and report non-increasing times and duplicate keys.

For duplicate `(ID, timestamp)` keys:

- Preserve every source record.
- Collapse exact duplicates only in the cleaned analytical view, retaining all source references.
- Quarantine conflicting duplicates unless an authoritative priority rule resolves them.
- Do not average conflicting positions or select an arbitrary record.

### Positions

Decode documented fills and validate the native coordinate convention before longitude normalization. For example, an invalid longitude such as `9999` must not become a legal coordinate through modulo arithmetic.

Keep normalized coordinates as derived fields. Reject nonfinite or demonstrably invalid coordinates from position-dependent uses; retain their source records.

A coastline mismatch is a separate geometry issue, not automatic proof of an erroneous position or beaching.

### Velocity

Missing `u` or `v` does not automatically invalidate a position pair.

Retain legitimate zero velocities and negative vector components.

If calculating coordinate-based displacement, use actual elapsed time and a documented geodesic method. Label it as interval-averaged net displacement velocity, distinct from the official hourly velocity estimate.

Do not require reconstructed velocity as a prerequisite for position-based transitions.

### Temperature

Missing or unusable SST does not automatically invalidate an otherwise usable position or core transition.

For this product, verify and implement the recommended total/non-diurnal SST quality policy using flags 4/5 and the documented −2 to 50°C range; use the separate diurnal-component policy, including the v2.01 correction. Keep temperature rejection separate from position rejection. Flag 4 does not mean its uncertainty should be replaced with zero.

Distinguish absolute temperature from temperature anomalies: subtract 273.15 when converting absolute Kelvin SST to Celsius; do not subtract it from an SST anomaly. Negative anomalies may be legitimate. Bind these rules to the [SST paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-022-01670-2.pdf) and [release corrections](https://www.aoml.noaa.gov/phod/gdp/hourly_data.php).

### Uncertainty and metadata

Missing uncertainty is not zero uncertainty.

For suspicious negative uncertainty values, verify whether they are documented special codes or unresolved source anomalies. Do not take absolute values or invalidate positions solely because the associated uncertainty needs investigation.

Preserve location type, hardware type, drogue depth, experiment/deployment information, death codes and other relevant metadata.

Never interpret a death code, missing transmission or final recorded position as physical exit or confirmed beaching without the required evidence.

## 7. Investigate extreme speeds and jumps through multiple evidence sources

An extreme-speed or jump flag is a request for investigation, not an automatic deletion rule.

Use this diagnostic sequence:

1. **Source and calculation checks:** original values, units, timestamp decoding, actual elapsed time, duplicate keys, fill decoding and longitude normalization.
2. **Within-track checks:** previous/current/next positions, velocities, reversals, isolated spikes and temporal consistency.
3. **Measurement context:** location type, uncertainty, original sampling indicators and regime metadata.
4. **Contemporaneous peer checks:** comparable observations from other drifter IDs.
5. **Bounded spatial/temporal extension:** nearby wet-connected locations, with changing comparability explicitly recorded.
6. **Evidence-based disposition:** retain with flags, correct a derived calculation, invalidate an affected field, or mark a particular use unresolved.

Official velocities and coordinate-derived velocities are related processed quantities. Agreement is useful corroboration; it is not independent proof. Their disagreement may reflect different temporal averaging.

Do not impose one universal ocean-speed ceiling or remove fast-current observations because they disagree with a regional mean.

Statistical thresholds must have a recorded source or an explicitly declared development-only screening rationale. Screening thresholds may prioritize review; they must not silently become deletion thresholds.

Consider wet connectivity, coastal barriers, fronts, differing regimes and available depth/hardware information. Nearby observations are not automatically exchangeable.

If contemporaneous peers are unavailable, record `peer_unavailable`. Same-season historical observations may provide a labelled background comparison, but cannot verify the missing state of that particular event.

## 8. Use correlation and reconstruction without creating false evidence

We want to exploit useful spatial and temporal relationships efficiently. Separate three activities:

### A. Recover authentic source information

Check whether an apparent missing record is a retrieval/export defect.

### B. Diagnose and annotate

Use the drifter’s own trajectory, comparable other drifters and documented metadata to assess plausibility and missingness mechanisms.

These checks can improve flags and explain uncertainty. They do not automatically create an observed position.

### C. Experimentally reconstruct

If justified after the core audit, run a bounded reconstruction diagnostic in a separate data layer.

For every estimate, record:

- Method and configuration.
- Input/reference IDs and timestamps.
- Information availability.
- Whether both sides of the gap were used.
- Estimated value or distribution.
- Uncertainty and competing plausible paths.
- Validation status and source linkage.

Validate using withheld known development records, realistic gap patterns and whole-ID separation. Examine displacement error, uncertainty coverage, cell assignment and exit-event errors across gap lengths, regimes and coastal/branch conditions.

Using both sides of a gap is retrospective reconstruction; do not represent it as causal forecasting.

A masking experiment does not prove validity for genuine missing events whose missingness mechanism differs.

Reconstructed records must not:

- Overwrite source values.
- Become primary observed endpoints or first-exit evidence.
- Increase independent drifter counts.
- Validate a model against its own reconstructed labels.
- Manufacture constant-regime status or complete paths.
- Enter the primary transition counts without an explicit, versioned architecture decision.

Possible future use as imputed features or training augmentation must specify a different validated protocol. Reconstruction is not a compulsory dependency for the core position-based preparation.

### Endpoint occupancy

Require a usable origin, usable matched endpoint and confirmed scenario eligibility through the relevant interval.

An intervening position gap does not automatically invalidate the endpoint pair if regime constancy remains independently confirmable.

Do not infer constant regime merely because the observed flags on either side of a gap match.

### Stopped-first-exit target

Require every expected QC-valid hourly position and the declared regime through:

`min(horizon, first validly established observed exit)`

Scan the required interval chronologically.

- A gap or unusable position before exit makes the path classification unresolved.
- An observed exit after such a gap does not retrospectively establish an eligible first exit.
- A complete eligible path to an observed exit establishes the absorbing outside outcome for subsequent horizons.
- Later return, termination, missing endpoints, regime changes or source cutoff do not erase that already established stopped outcome.
- Without an established exit, require the complete eligible path through the horizon.

This is first exit observed on the hourly grid, not exact physical first exit between observations.

Keep endpoint occupancy and stopped/path ledgers distinct. Comparisons between models must use the same target and eligible cases.

### Missingness, censoring and outcome reasons

Separate:

- Invalid/unresolved origin.
- Invalid/unresolved endpoint.
- Missing target timestamp.
- Administrative immaturity.
- Track termination.
- Unknown or switching regime.
- Incomplete path.
- Observed physical-domain exit.
- Geometry/state-mapping uncertainty.

Administrative immaturity means the requested target exceeds the source cutoff; termination within available source time is different.

Calculate eligibility predicates first. Then apply a published deterministic primary-reason precedence for count reconciliation, retaining all secondary flags. Known eligible stopped exit takes precedence over later immaturity or termination.

Missing outcomes are not observed destinations. Do not count unknown future positions as physical outside or as observed transitions to the model’s unresolved-support state.
